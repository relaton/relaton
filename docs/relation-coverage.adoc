= Relation coverage in the published corpora
v1.0 (2026-10-20)

What the published data actually carries, per SDO: how many records state a
relation, which relation types dominate, and how many carry a publication
status. It is the "what exists" half of the ingestion picture; the sources'
capability to volunteer this data is in `docs/source-capability-matrix.adoc`.

Method: a line scan of every `relaton-data-*` checkout in the workspace on the
date above, counting top-level `relation:` entries by `type` and
`status.stage` values. Counts are relation *entries*, not distinct targets. The
three repos not checked out or empty locally (Adobe, IANA, OMG) are not
measured; their published indexes exist (31, 3,405 and 256 rows respectively).

Totals: **552,484 records; 234,656 (42%) state at least one relation;
216,556 (39%) carry a status.**

== What the numbers say

* **Relation coverage is bimodal.** Fifteen flavors volunteer relations at
  meaningful density; eleven carry none at all. There is very little middle
  ground, so an adoption-chain or graph feature either has data in a flavor or
  has nothing to work with — it cannot assume a relation exists because the
  SDO publishes related documents.
* **The largest graph is not the largest corpus.** IETF (177,559 records,
  141,647 with relations) and ISO (61,635 / 41,505) are dense; ITU has the
  highest entries-per-record (~20 relation entries per record, 17 of them
  `complementOf`), while ETSI (28,663), IEC (32,835), GOST (42,112) and JIS
  (21,482) carry **no relations at all**.
* **Status and relations are independent.** ETSI, IEC, GOST, JIS and EASC
  publish status without relations; ITU, NIST, W3C and IEEE publish relations
  without a top-level status. A consumer that needs both must combine sources.
* **Adoption edges are scarce.** `adoptedAs` appears only in 3GPP (29) and
  CCSDS (310); `adoptedFrom` only in IEEE (115). Cross-SDO adoption chains
  (ISO → EN → BS EN) are not recoverable from the published corpora alone —
  they need either source enrichment or an equivalence table.
* **ITU-R volunteers neither.** Its crawler scrapes a status but deliberately
  does not model it (the preserved dataset carried none; see the parser spec),
  and the server-rendered pages expose no relation links. The 0/0 row is a
  modelling decision on the status side and a source limit on the relation
  side.

== Caveats

* A relation entry is not a verified edge: targets are not resolved, and a
  dangling or wrong target counts the same as a valid one. Graph integrity
  (adoption chains, bidirectional edges, family views) is a separate audit.
* Counts are per published file, not per index row; a consolidated edition and
  its records can differ, which is why ETSI's file count and index-row count
  differ.
* Adobe, IANA and OMG are absent because their local checkouts were empty or
  missing at audit time.
